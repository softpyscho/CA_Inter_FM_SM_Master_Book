PAPER – 5: AUDITING AND ETHICS 
Specific Comments 
Question 1.(a)  Most appropriate type of audit evidence in verification of 
an assertion: Most of the examinees emphasised on the sufficiency and 
appropriateness of audit evidence instead of identifying the best evidence for 
testing assertions. Majority of examinees could not identify the evidence that 
brings the highest satisfaction to the auditor about the appropriateness or 
otherwise of the assertion. However, they correctly wrote the most suitable 
evidence type for verifying cash and investments pledged with the bank.  
(b) Objective of identifying and assessing the risk of material 
misstatement: Many examinees did not explain how the auditor will satisfy 
the objective of identifying and assessing the risk of material misstatement. 
Instead, they described the components of risk of material misstatements and 
incorrectly described the risk assessment procedures. 
(c) Additional audit procedures for inventory verification on a date other 
than the balance sheet date: Most of the examinees did not correctly 
describe the audit procedures applicable when inventory is verified on a date 
other than the date of the ﬁnancial statements. Many examinees did not 
address the requirement to verify whether the changes in inventory between 
the physical count date and the financial statement date have been accurately

'Significant deficiency in internal control': Most of the examinees could not

(b)  Purpose of communicating Key Audit Matters and Key Audit Matters 
not a substitute for disclosure in financial statements: Many examinees 
wrote an incomplete understanding of the purpose of communicating key 
audit matters as given in SA 701. Many examinees reproduced general 
statements about audit reporting without explaining how key audit matters 
enhance the communicative value of the auditor’s report or assists users in 
understanding matters of most significance in the audit. 
In addition, some examinees did not explain properly that communicating key 
audit matters is not a substitute for disclosure in the financial statements etc. 
Instead of identifying the specific points required by the Standard, several 
examinees wrote the definition of Key audit matters itself which was not 
required.  
Question 3.(a) Applicability of Reporting requirements on adequacy of 
IFC and Applicability of CARO to a Private Limited Company: Most of the 
examinees were unable to accurately state the conditions under which 
reporting on the adequacy and operating effectiveness of Internal Financial 
Controls (IFC) is not applicable to a private limited company. Many examinees 
reproduced incomplete conditions or confused the exemption available to 
private companies with general reporting requirements. Several examinees 
omitted key thresholds relating to turnover and aggregate borrowings, which 
are essential for determining the exemption. 
With respect to the applicability of CARO, 2020, many examinees listed the 
criteria incorrectly and mixed up the financial limits. Some examinees failed to 
mention that all three conditions—paid-up capital and reserves limit, 
borrowing limit, and total revenue limit—must be satisfied cumulatively.  
(b) Factors that may make it appropriate to revise the terms of the audit 
engagement: Many examinees were unable to clearly identify the specific 
situations in which the auditor should consider revising the terms of an audit

(c) Meaning and objective of Take-out finance and income recognition

infrastructure projects, addressing exposure and asset–liability mismatch 
issues and increasing the availability of longer-tenor debt. Instead, many 
examinees provided irrelevant points or broadly described benefits of bank 
loans, which were not required.

engagement: Some examinees demonstrated an inadequate understanding

(c) Statistical Sampling - More scientific approach to Sampling: Many 
examinees were unable to clearly state why statistical sampling is considered 
more scientific than judgmental sampling. Instead of discussing the use of 
probability theory, the absence of auditor bias and ability to project results to

“more accurate” or “more reliable” without explaining the underlying 
reasons. 
Many examinees did not apply the concept to the case involving over one 
lakh sales transactions. Also, they did not mention that statistical sampling is 
particularly useful in large and homogeneous populations. Some examinees 
incorrectly suggested non-statistical or judgement-based methods as the 
preferred approach. 
Further, many examinees confused type of sampling describing haphazard or 
judgmental selection instead of random sampling, which is the appropriate 
method in such a situation.  
Question 5.(a) Factors for planning the nature, timing, and extent of 
direction and supervision of the engagement team members and review 
of their work: Most of the examinees recognised the relevant factors and 
were able to list the required points accurately. In the second part, many 
examinees appropriately referred to modifications in the audit plan, audit 
strategy or audit procedures. However, examinees did not explain that the 
audit plan must be updated when new information or unexpected events 
occur.  
(b) Audit Procedures for verification of the assertion "Existence" in case 
of Borrowings: Only few examinees understood audit procedures relating to 
verification of borrowings. Some examinees did not mention essential 
procedures such as reviewing board minutes for approval of borrowings, 
examining compliance with covenants, verifying charges on assets (including

(c) Auditor's Duty Regarding Audit of LLP: Many examinees lacked a

Question 6.(a) Disclosures for inventories under Schedule III (Part I) to 
the Companies Act, 2013: Some examinees failed to answer as required 
and instead wrote generally about audit procedures for inventory 
verification, such as physical counting or valuation checks. This reflected a 
lack of clarity between audit procedures and statutory disclosure 
requirements under Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013. 
Few examinees did not give the specific classification - Raw Materials, WIP, 
Finished Goods, Stock-in-Trade, Stores & Spares, Loose Tools, and Others as 
well as omitting the requirement to disclose goods-in-transit separately 
under the respective heads.  
(b) Audit of stores and inventories of the Government Department: 
Many examinees did not write the specific issues faced by government 
departments and instead wrote general procedures relating to physical 
verification or valuation of inventories. Examinees often overlooked key 
aspects such as assessing compliance with government purchase regulations, 
identifying uneconomical or excessive inventory holdings and examining 
reasonableness of prices and contract terms.  
(c) OR Classification of Appropriate Threats with Reasons 
Examinees correctly identified the types of threats. However, a few were 
unable to provide the correct reasoning for their classification.
